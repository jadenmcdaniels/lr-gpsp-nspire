Bluetooth controllers and headphones
====================================

What it takes
-------------
A USB Bluetooth dongle on the calculator's USB port (an OTG adapter or a
USB hub on it, as for a USB controller). The tested design is the TP-Link
UB500 (2357:0604, Realtek RTL8761BU). A Realtek dongle needs its firmware,
uploaded at every power-up, from two files in My Documents/zgpSP_files/bt/:

    rtl8761bu_fw.bin.tns      (44484 bytes, linux-firmware's rtl8761bu_fw.bin)
    rtl8761bu_config.bin.tns  (6 bytes, linux-firmware's rtl8761bu_config.bin)

Without them the dongle runs on its ROM code and may not work; the
Bluetooth page says the firmware file is missing. CSR (0a12:0001) and
Broadcom dongles need no file. The paired devices are kept in
zgpSP_files/bt/bt_keys.dat.tns (written from the menus, never mid-game).

Settings > Bluetooth (the page is ui7's: nspire_devices.c)
--------------------
  Bluetooth       On (the default) or Off (enter flips it). On: the driver
                  waits for a dongle and costs nothing until one comes.
  Search          The page searches as it opens; enter searches again.
  PAIRED          The paired devices: enter connects or disconnects, del
                  forgets (asked first).
  FOUND           Everything heard nearby, nothing left out: controllers
                  and headphones first, then the rest, each with its kind
                  ("WEAK" after it when its signal is: under -75 dBm, the
                  lowest two of four steps; nothing for a normal one);
                  enter pairs and connects the picked one.
  A device being paired or connected says PAIRING or CONNECTING, and the
  band ENTER STOP: enter on it again stops it (a misclick undone: the band
  says STOPPED., the device is found, or paired, as it was before), enter
  once more starts it again. One connect at a time: a second one asked
  waits its turn (CONNECTING meanwhile); the last headphones' automatic
  reconnect gives way to the one you asked for, and stopping it on their
  line keeps it from trying again until you connect them (or they connect
  by themselves).
  The band says what is going on: a pairing's outcome, the dongle
  missing, its firmware file missing, Bluetooth stopped.

Pairing mode: a DualSense: hold Create and PS until its light flashes; a
Switch Pro Controller: hold the sync button on its top; a DualShock 4:
Share and PS; an Xbox Wireless Controller (Bluetooth LE: the Series pads,
the One S and Elite 2 with their 2021 firmware): turn it on with the Xbox
button, then hold the pair button on its top (by the USB port) until the
Xbox button flashes fast. A paired controller turned on later connects by
itself (an LE one when it advertises: gpSP waits for it at a low duty).
Bluetooth LE controllers work as the Classic ones (the same slots, toasts,
numbers, Settings > Keys; the Xbox pad's A is A and it is named "Xbox One"
as on USB). An LE device that is not a controller is not paired with:
"Only controllers and headphones connect." If LE does not work on the
dongle, its LE controllers' lines say "This dongle can't connect LE
controllers." and everything else goes on as before.
The last headphones used connect when Bluetooth comes on (tried four times
in a minute; a failed try says nothing).

Controllers
-----------
A Bluetooth controller works as a USB one does (the same buttons, layout
lines in gpsp_usbpad.cfg.tns, the Controls screen). Two at once. They are
numbered after the highest USB slot in use (the USB controllers keep their
slots): a USB controller and a DualSense are Controller 1 and Controller 2
("Controller 2 connected (Bluetooth)"); with only USB slot 2 in use, the
DualSense is Controller 3. A controller in or out (USB or Bluetooth)
renumbers them; the pads' lights show the same number, the Controller page
lists a line per USB slot and each Bluetooth one by that number
("Controller 2 · Bluetooth  DualSense"). All drive the GBA's one player.

Sound
-----
Settings > Sound > Output: Automatic (every output that is in: Bluetooth
headphones and a USB sound adapter both play; the adapter's sound is held
back to come out with the headphones', which are ~0.27 s late by their own
buffering: what their ring and lead measure, averaged, and their A2DP
delay report, else an estimate of 150 ms for the earbuds' part; a move of
that delay held 2 s, or a gap in the game's sound, re-syncs the adapter
with a short fade and a moment of silence), USB adapter (the adapter only:
the headphones stay connected, paused, nothing encoded), or Bluetooth (the
headphones only: an adapter is idle, nothing sent to it, when its driver
has the idle mode; the Sound page says "USB audio idle (Output
Bluetooth)"). The Sound page's band says "USB audio + Bluetooth:
NAME" while both play. BT sound: Automatic (mono on a CX, stereo on a CX
II: SBC encoding costs a CX about 5% of its CPU in mono), Mono, Stereo
(bitpool 35, A2DP's "middle quality") or High quality (bitpool 53, what
phones send: clearer highs, 69 packets a second to the dongle instead of
43); a change takes effect at the headphones' next connect. Paused in the
menus, sped up in fast forward, as the USB adapter's.

Logs
----
gpsp_bt.txt.tns (zgpSP_files, written at the exit once a dongle was seen):
the dongle's USB attach, its firmware, every HCI command and event in short,
searches, pairing, the controllers' set-up, the headphones' stream, the
stop; while headphones stream, a line a second at its end (the frame's
USB interrupt window, the poll and the sound's write in ms; media packets,
SBC frames, the ring, the rate, late frames, underruns, trims).
gpsp_pad.txt.tns has Bluetooth's lines too (on, off, controllers and
headphones coming and going, the stop). gpsp_bt.txt.tns is written on the
exit's restart path too (a USB shutdown that timed out).

Exit and failures
-----------------
quit() writes the key store first (the stop writes no file), then gives
Bluetooth half of what the USB shutdowns have left (1.5 s at most): under
600 ms nothing is asked of the radio (the links end with the dongle's
power); a USB callback still inside gpSP then means the exit's restart. An
exit() that didn't come through quit() stops Bluetooth before the pad
driver (its handler is registered after the pad driver's). Every wait is
bounded by polls or sleeps as well as by time (an older CX's timer may not
run). A BTstack assert (an internal check) gives the dongle up: "Bluetooth
stopped", the game goes on, the dongle no longer counts as a working
device (a replug or the port's restart starts over); one with no library
call to return to writes the game's save and the logs, then restarts the
calculator as an exit that timed out. Leaving Settings > Sound applies the
codec only: a Bluetooth that failed stays stopped until turned on again.

Build
-----
The Makefile builds ../nspire-bt's library (make -C ../nspire-bt lib: the
profiles, BTstack 1.8.3, the calculator port; -Os, the SBC encoder -O2) and
links it when ../nspire-bt is there; BT=0 (or no ../nspire-bt) builds
without Bluetooth (-DNSPIRE_NO_BT). The one nspire-usbshim is gpSP's.
Options: BT_LE=full (the default since r19-bt: LE controllers connect;
+25.0 KB of gpsp.tns against scan), scan (a search lists Bluetooth LE
devices, none connects), off (Classic only); BT_AVRCP=0 (the default: the
headphones' volume in the samples) or 1 (absolute volume, the headphones'
keys: +10.0 KB).

The switch file zgpSP_files/bt/bt_port.cfg.tns (optional; nspire-bt
docs/calc-port.md): requeue=main|callback, out=handover|main,
intr_recover=on|off, log_level=0|1|2, for a run that compares the USB
transport's two ways on the calculator without a rebuild.

Tests
-----
tests/host/bt_tests.py (in tests/host/run.sh) on host_bt.c, a stand-in for
the library: no dongle costs nothing, the pads' numbers (USB slots 0/1), the
sound's routing (Output exclusive), the page, the settings, the exit (its
order, a stop that spends time, a polite part, a stopped timer, the
restart's path with gpsp_bt.txt.tns), an assert under a guard and the fatal
hook, the poll in the frames' OS time, the headphones' delay and the rate
in gpsp_bt.txt.tns; tests/audio/sink_test.c: Output on the USB adapter's
side, its re-syncs to the headphones, its idle; tests/audio/pace_test.c
--case idle: what the idle adapter saves. tests/host/ui_wired.sh OUT bluetooth: the
page's screens. tests/bt/btbench.py: what Bluetooth costs a frame on the
calculator's CPU (the speed model). nspire-bt's own tests cover the library.
Not verified on a calculator.
