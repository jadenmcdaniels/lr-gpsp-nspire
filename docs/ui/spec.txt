gpSP for the TI-Nspire: the user interface ("plain")
====================================================

r19 (2026-11-21) changed the screens' structure, the keys in the menus, the settings and the Keys page:
docs/ui/r19.txt is how it is now. Where the two differ, r19.txt holds. What stands from here: the look (1,
3, 4), the grid's lines and band (2, with the left edge now 8 for everything and the tabs' row at the top),
the battery's reading (9).

Everything below is drawn by the real code (nspire_ui.c, nspire_ui_screens.c,
nspire_osd.c; the menus' tree and what the keys do are gui.c's).
tests/host/ui_wired.sh OUTDIR draws every screen on the PC through that code;
its "court" scene draws the main ones with real games (court-<what it
shows>.png; the scene kept its name from the look before).

1. WHAT IT IS BUILT ON
----------------------
A white page, black type in one monospaced face at one size, things shown as
a grid of pictures with a short name under each, a top row of a few words
instead of menus, nothing decorated, no notes. The menus follow from it:
  - The games are a grid, three by two, each its latest save's picture with
    its name under it. Only the picked game is in its own colours; the others
    are black and white prints, so the one colour on a screen is the game
    you are on.
  - The paused menu isn't a list: the picture is in the middle of the
    screen, the choices are one row along its bottom.
  - A settings page is a list (r16): a line each, its name at the left,
    its value at the right; up/down move, left/right change the value.
  - A line's name and its value say what it is, the value with its unit or
    its meaning ("15 presses/s", "4 in a row", "Auto: 396 MHz", "No
    limit"; never a bare number or a word that needs the manual). What a
    setting does is said once, under the settings page's grid, in one or
    two plain lines for the picked cell. What has to be said elsewhere is
    said in a few capital words ("NO CABLE", "PICK LINK ON BOTH").

2. GRID
-------
(r16, nspire_ui.h "The grid".) 320 x 240, one left edge
(x 14), as far in on the right; two columns of 142, 8 apart (the second
from x 164); three of 97 (the game list). Every screen is three parts in
the same places:
  header       small words on baseline 20, no rule: the title, where
               it is opened from before it in grey, a note and the
               battery at the right; the paused menu's game name too
  body         from row 36 in lines of 18 (a line's text 13 under its
               top); a settings cell is two lines (36, its block 34);
               at most ten lines (to row 216)
  band         rows 222-239, one line on baseline 235: what just
               happened, else the picked thing's one sentence, else the
               keys that aren't obvious; the paused menu's row of
               choices is its band
  game list    pictures 88 x 59 at rows 34 and 118, a name on one line
               under each (cut "..."); the picked game's whole name and
               its facts on the body's line 9
  paused menu  the paused game 240 x 160 at (40, 36), a saved picture
               144 x 96 in the middle of the same place; what it is on
               the body's line 9 (baseline 211); Settings: its pages as
               a list from row 36
  settings     a list from row 36: the name left, the value right

3. TYPE
-------
IBM Plex Mono (Regular and Medium), one size: 11 px. Medium for names and
choices, Regular for the rest; capitals for names, tabs and choices. The
headers too (until 2026-11-17 the screens' titles were the big words, twice
the height of what was under them; now they are the size of the rest), and
a question's title. docs/fonts/README.md says where it comes from and how the glyphs
are made.

4. COLOUR
---------
Black and white. A white ground, black type; no rules or frames where
whitespace and alignment do the job (r16).
The picked thing is a black block with its type in white. Values and
facts are black (r16). One dark grey (#545454), only for what is
deliberately in the back: the tab you are not on and where a page is
opened from, a list's section names, the names of keys that do nothing
on the keypad drawing, what isn't there. No light grey anywhere.

5. COMPONENTS
-------------
  tab          a word in capitals; the one you are on black, the others grey
  picked       a black block, its type in white (a choice in the row, the
               picked game's name, the picked cell, the picked list line)
  answer       a word in the band; the picked one a black block (r16:
               a question's answers are as the paused menu's choices)
  key chip     a black chip with the key's name in white (the band at the
               bottom); on the Keys page an outlined chip, white on the
               picked line's block
  picture      a game's own picture; one not picked: in black and white;
               none: the game's initial alone, or EMPTY, no frame
  progress     an outline 6 high, filled black as far as it has got

6. THE SCREENS
--------------
  Games        tabs GAMES and FOLDERS (r16: no counts), the battery at
               the right; the grid; the picked game's whole name on the
               line over the band, its last save and how many saves at
               that line's right; past six games, where these are along
               the right edge.
  Paused       which game at the top left, the battery at the right; the
               picture in the middle; the row in the band: Resume, Save,
               Load, Link, Games, Settings, Quit. Save goes back to the
               game (r16). Settings: its pages listed in the middle, each
               with what it holds now; up/down pick one, enter opens it
               (no Settings screen of its own).
               Resume: the paused game as it was, 240 x 160 pixel for
               pixel, its last save on the line under it; up/down:
               another game's saved picture as it is, its name and when
               it was played under it; while there are others, "^ 2/5 v"
               at the top right (r16).
               Save, Load: the slot's picture, its neighbours small either
               side; "SLOT-07" and its age under it.
               Link: the two calculators and the cable; the state, and what
               to do, in a few words.
  Settings     a list. Pages: Sound, Controller, (Bluetooth), Keys,
               Picture, Speed, Saving, Link, (Cheats), System (About gpSP
               and the rest). A page or a thing to do is a line like the
               others, its name and what it holds or what enter does
               ("Speed test  Run, 25 s", "About  gpSP, credits ->").
  Starting     the header (STARTING); the game's picture where the paused
               menu has it, its name in capitals under it, the bar, how
               far ("2 OF 4 MB").
  In a game    the speed readout at the top left, each number after its
               name and a colon: "FPS: 60", "FPS: 60  DRAWN: 54",
               "FPS: 60.0  CPU: 11.5 GFX: 0.1 OUT: 2.7 IDLE: 2.3";
               messages at the bottom band's left corner, in capitals,
               white on black, up for their reading time (r16).
  Keys         the list of buttons with their keys, the calculator's keypad
               or the controller beside it: keys that do something outlined,
               the picked button's black.
  Questions    a screen of their own: the title and what happens on the
               white, the answers in the band (r16; until then a card
               over the screen it asked about); the safe answer picked
               first.

7. WHAT THE KEYS DO, EVERYWHERE
-------------------------------
  arrows        move on the screen as it is laid out: the grid in four
                directions; the paused menu's row left and right, up and
                down picking another game (Resume) or slot (Save, Load); a
                settings page's cells as a grid. Up or down past an edge
                goes to the other end of the same column (the game list:
                the same column of the first or last row; a shorter row
                under it: its last game)
  a long text   a name or value too long for its place ends in "...";
                in the picked line or cell it waits a second, then
                scrolls to its end
  enter         does the picked thing: opens it, flips it, starts changing
                it (then left/right change it, enter keeps it, esc puts it
                back)
  esc, menu     back one step; from the paused menu, to the game
  The keys on A and B are enter and esc too. A controller's D-pad, A and B
  do the same. 8 2 5 4 6 are the arrows in every menu (the game list too);
  no number key starts, loads or picks anything (until 2026-11-17 1-9 and
  0 started the list's first ten games).

8. SIZE
-------
ARM -O3 (nspire-gcc, the calculator's flags), text + data.

9. THE BATTERY: HOW A PROGRAM CAN READ IT
-----------------------------------------
What is known
  * Neither Ndless nor the OS gives programs a battery call. The SDK headers
    (Ndless/ndless-sdk/include) have nothing; the OS has an internal
    read_adc (named in Ndless's IDC files for CX OS 4.5.4 at 0x100DD630 and
    4.5.5 at 0x100DD5F0), not a syscall and not named for any CX II OS.
    The percentage the OS shows on its status screen is its own sum.
  * CX (and CX CAS, older non-CX II): the ADC is at 0xC4000000 (Firebird
    core/misc.c, mem.c). Seven channels, channel n's registers at
    0xC4000100 + n * 0x20: write 1 to +0x00 to measure, the 10-bit result
    at +0x10 (+0x04, +0x08, +0x0C, +0x14: setup, count, DMA address,
    speed). Interrupt status / mask at 0xC4000004 / 0xC4000008. Scale:
    155 units a volt on channels 1 and 2, 310 on the others; 0x3FF when the
    ADC's clock is off (0x900B0020 bit 4, the CX's power controller).
    Channel 3 is the keypad type (about 73 for a touchpad keypad).
    Firebird answers 930 on every other channel, so it does not say which
    one carries the battery (VBATT or VSYS); on the old AAA models it was a
    155-a-volt channel. 930 at 155 a volt is 6.0 V (AAA x4), at 310 3.0 V.
  * CX II: 0x900B0000 is the ADC (Firebird's map; on the CX it is the power
    controller, nspire_clock.c). Firebird does not model it (reads
    0x6969, raises the ADC interrupt on any write), so neither its
    registers nor its scale are known. The power controller is at
    0x90140000 (nspire_clock.c, nspire_os.c log it); no battery register
    is known there either.
  * Charging: no charger status is known on either model. A computer or a
    charger on the USB port is visible as something on the port at the
    device's end (nspire_usb_plugged, the OTG ID bit in nspire_usb_bits);
    that is "on USB power", not "charging", and a link cable to the other
    calculator must not count. UNVERIFIED.

What must be measured on two calculators (a CX and a CX II)
  1. A small Ndless test program (not gpSP) that, every 2 s, reads
     (CX) all seven ADC channels at 0xC4000100 + n * 0x20, and (CX II) the
     words at 0x900B0000-0x900B00FF before and after writing 1 to each
     word that looks like a channel's command register, and appends them
     to a file with the time.
  2. Run it at three charge levels the OS reports (about 90%, 50% and
     15%: the OS's status screen, written down at the same time), once on
     battery and once with the USB cable to a computer in.
  3. The channel whose value falls with the OS's percentage is the battery;
     its three readings against the OS's three percentages give the curve
     (a Li-ion cell: roughly 3.4 V empty to 4.1-4.2 V full, not linear;
     map it piecewise between the measured points). The USB-in readings
     show how far charging lifts the reading, and whether some bit tells
     charging.

How the number behaves (the engineer's rules)
  * Read at most once every 5 s, only while a menu is on the screen (never
    in the game loop: no cost a frame); five readings make the first value.
  * Smooth: the median of the last five readings, then a slow average
    (each new value moves it 1/8 of the way).
  * Show whole percents, but only move the shown figure when the smoothed
    one is 2 points away, or has stayed 1 point away for a minute: no
    82-81-82 flicker. On battery the figure never goes up; on USB power
    it never goes down.
  * Low below 15%, with the same 2-point margin to go back.
  * Unknown (an unmeasured model, a reading of 0 or 0x3FF, or out of the
    measured range): ui_battery = -1 and the header shows nothing. A
    battery that is wrong is worse than none.
